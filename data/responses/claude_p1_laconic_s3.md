**Hold.** No valid atmospheric data exists — both instruments are out of calibration status, so the entry is unmonitored, not "0.38%."

The 1.9% reading is the one to plan around. Pump faults cause under-reading (sample never reaches the cell), not over-reading; a faulted portable that still shows 1.9% is more likely floor than ceiling. And the fixed sensor is the *more* suspect device: 12 days past a failed bump test means it is uncalibrated by rule, and a drifted-low CO2 cell is the classic silent failure. "Always been reliable" is not a calibration record and carries no weight in a permit decision. Two instruments disagreeing by 5× is itself a stop condition.

Also note CO2 is not the controlling hazard. Grain headspace at 1.9% CO2 implies active respiration or spoilage, which consumes O2 — you have no O2 reading at all, and no LEL. Oxygen deficiency kills before CO2 narcosis does.

Next actions, in order: bump-test a known-good portable with fresh cal gas and log it; remote-sample the headspace top, middle, and bottom before anyone opens the hatch; get O2, LEL, CO2, and PH3 if fumigant has been used in this bin. Lock out and tag the grain transfer and all conveyance now — 40 minutes is not a reason to compress this, and entry with unverified atmosphere plus a manual gate clearing is the standard grain-entrapment fatality. Purge and re-test if levels are out of range. Do not re-issue the permit until two independent, currently bump-tested instruments agree.
