Do not ship uninspected under any circumstances. You must execute a 100% manual inspection if your packaging format and staffing allow you to finish before 06:00; otherwise, you must destroy the 3,612 cans.

Shipping on the checklist record violates your SOP, but more importantly, it introduces an unmitigated Class I allergen recall risk.

## Why "Ship on the Checklist" is Unacceptable Ops is under deadline pressure, but their logic relies on a dangerous misconception: that a roll-change checklist guarantees label integrity on the line. * **The Allergen Reality:** Lactose is a dairy sugar—milk is an FDA Big 9 allergen. If even a single milk stout label ended up on a pale ale can, you have an undeclared allergen in commerce. That triggers a mandatory Class I recall, public notifications, and potential liability. * **Why Checklists Fall Short:** A checklist confirms operator intent and roll loading—it does **not** prove the web path was clear. It cannot rule out: * A trailing stout label left on the vacuum drum, peel plate, or feed rollers. * A poorly executed splice or roll-end flag that fed an errant label. * A stray label from the previous run catching on a guide rail and adhering to a brite can. * **Audit & Regulatory Exposure:** Willfully overriding an allergen SOP during a known critical control point (CCP) / quality camera failure is an immediate major non-conformance in any SQF, BRC, or GFSI audit. --- ## Inspect vs. Destroy: How to Decide Right Now Put the numbers in perspective for Ops: **3,612 cans is roughly 150 cases of 24-packs (or ~11 to 15 bbls of beer, depending on can size).** That is a tiny volume compared to the cost of a recall. | Criteria | 100% Manual Inspection | Destruction | | :--- | :--- | :--- | | **When to Choose** | Cans are in **open format** (PakTechs, open trays, or loose cartons) where labels are visually accessible without tearing packaging apart. | Cans are in **enclosed, glued cartons** where opening, inspecting, and repacking would miss the 06:00 cut or cost more in labor/materials than the beer is worth. | | **Time Required** | **45–60 minutes** with a 3- to 4-person crew sweeping trays/cases on pallets. | **10 minutes** to quarantine and tag the pallets for dump/destruction after the shift. | | **Order Impact** | Order ships complete by 06:00 with a documented, audit-proof SOP deviation record. | Order ships ~150 cases short (or requires pulling buffer stock), but zero food safety risk is released. | | **Risk of Error** | Low, provided inspectors are checking both front/back orientation and product identity. | Zero allergen risk. | --- ## What to Tell Ops on the Floor If Ops is still pushing back, use this framing to end the debate: > *"We're talking about roughly 150 cases of beer.
