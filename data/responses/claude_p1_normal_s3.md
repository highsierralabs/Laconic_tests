**Hold.** This isn't a close call, and the reason isn't the disagreement between the two readings — it's that you don't actually have two readings.

**The fixed sensor's 0.38% is not data.** An instrument that fails a bump test is out of service from the moment of that failure until it's calibrated and re-verified. Twelve days of readings since then are unverified output, not measurements. "Has always been reliable" is a statement about the past of an instrument that has now demonstrably stopped being reliable — it's the exact reasoning that shows up in the causal chain of confined-space fatality reports. So the comparison isn't 0.38% vs. 1.9%. It's 1.9% vs. nothing.

**And the pump faults cut the wrong way for comfort.** A pump or flow fault on an aspirated instrument almost always biases *low* — restricted sample delivery means the sensor sees less than the true atmosphere. If the 1.9% is suspect, the plausible direction of error is that the headspace is worse, not better. 1.9% is already ~4x your action limit, above the CO₂ STEL, and roughly half of IDLH.

**The bigger gap is what nobody mentioned: oxygen.** There's no O₂ reading in anything you've described. CO₂ at that level in a grain headspace implies active respiration or spoilage, which consumes O₂ as it produces CO₂ — the CO₂ number is a symptom, and the O₂ number is the one that kills people. You cannot issue an entry permit without a valid O₂ measurement, full stop. If the bin has been fumigated, you also need phosphine, and LEL if there's dust or fumigant in play.

**Separately, the task itself is the highest-fatality scenario in grain handling.** Manual clearing of a stuck gate means bridged or crusted grain, and entering to free a flow obstruction is the canonical entrapment death — atmosphere aside. The scheduled 14:00 transfer makes it worse, because it means there's an active reason for someone to energize fill or discharge equipment while a person is inside.

What needs to happen before anyone breaks the plane of that hatch:

- Fixed sensor tagged out of service; calibrated and verified before it's trusted again.
- A known-good portable — bump-tested that shift, flow/pump check passed — used for remote pre-entry testing through the hatch via sample tubing at multiple depths, top to bottom, since CO₂ stratifies and a single point reading tells you little.
- Full suite: O₂, CO₂, LEL, and fumigant if applicable.
- Mechanical ventilation, retest, and continuous monitoring during any occupancy.
- LOTO on all fill and discharge equipment, including whatever drives the 14:00 transfer.
- Attendant, retrieval harness and lifeline, and rescue capability on site — not "on call."
- Exhaust the non-entry options for the gate first: external vibration, long-handled tools from outside the space, vacuum. Most stuck gates can be cleared without a body inside.

On the 40 minutes: the transfer schedule is not a safety input. It's a cost of holding, and it belongs to whoever owns the schedule, not to the entry decision. Move the transfer.

I'm giving you the general reasoning, not a permit determination — that has to come from your qualified entry supervisor working your written program under OSHA 1910.146 and 1910.272. But if the question is whether the current evidence supports clearing entry, it doesn't come close.
