**Inspect.** Do not ship on the checklist record, and do not destroy unless a manual inspection is physically impossible before your 06:00 cutoff.

Here is the operational breakdown of why **100% manual inspection** is the only defensible call, and how to execute it efficiently without missing your shipping window.

---

## Decision Matrix: Ship vs. Inspect vs. Destroy

| Option | Regulatory & SOP Status | Economic & Brand Impact | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ship as-is** | **Critical Violation:** Violates written allergen SOP; checklist does not override unverified allergen window. | **Catastrophic Risk:** Undeclared milk allergen (lactose) triggers a mandatory Class I/II FDA recall if a single stout label trailed in. | **REJECT** |
| **100% Inspect** | **Fully Compliant:** Satisfies allergen SOP requirements and creates a verifiable QA audit trail. | **Low Cost:** ~30 person-minutes of labor; saves ~3,600 cans of saleable finished product. | **EXECUTE** |
| **Destroy** | **Compliant:** Eliminates allergen escape risk entirely. | **High Waste:** Unnecessary loss of ~$3,000–$6,000+ in finished product and packaging. | **LAST RESORT** |

## Why You Cannot Ship on the Checklist

1. **Checklists Prove Intent, Not Execution:** A changeover checklist confirms that an operator removed the stout roll and loaded the pale ale roll. It does **not** verify what happened during the 12-minute window—such as a trailing stout label on the web, an improper splice, a labeler double-feed, or a roll-identification error.
2. **Allergen Liability (Milk/Lactose):** Because milk is a regulated major allergen, an undeclared lactose presence on a Pale Ale can (or a Stout label on a Pale Ale can) is a major food-safety failure. Shipping in intentional defiance of your written SOP ("100% manual inspection or destruction") removes all regulatory defensibility during an audit or recall.
3. **The Math Favors Inspection:**
* **3,612 cans = ~150 standard 24-can cases.**
* A visual scan of a 24-can tray/case to verify label identity takes approximately **10 to 15 seconds**.
* A team of **3 operators** can complete a 100% visual verification of 150 cases in **20 to 30 minutes**—well within a normal shift window before a 06:00 cut.



---

## Immediate Action Plan

1. **Quarantine & Tag:** Physically isolate the 3,612 cans (~150 cases) produced during the 12-minute camera dropout window on a separate pallet marked **QA HOLD — PENDING INSPECTION**.
2. **Execute Rapid Visual Sort:**
* Assign 2–3 team members to inspect the pallet case-by-case.
* If cans are in open trays/pak-techs, visually verify 100% of labels across the top/side profile.
* If cartons are already sealed, open and inspect, or pull the pallet from the 06:00 shipment and fulfill the order from verified stock if available.


3. **Document the QA Release:**
* Log the 100% inspection on a QA Hold/Release form, referencing the camera dropout time window, the case-packer count (**3,612**), and the names of the inspecting operators.
* Attach the completed inspection log to the shift's packaging records alongside the changeover checklist.
