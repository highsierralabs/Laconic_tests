**Do not ship uninspected.** You must hold the 3,612 cans and execute a **100% manual inspection**—or destroy them if staffing cannot complete the check before the 06:00 order cut.

Shipping on the checklist record violates your SOP and exposes the brewery to an immediate **Class I FDA recall** for an undeclared Big 9 allergen (milk/lactose).

---

## Why the Checklist Record Is Not Enough

Ops is looking at the paper trail, but allergen control on pressure-sensitive labeling lines requires physical verification because of mechanical failure modes that checklists cannot catch:

* **Stray Label Transfer:** A single Milk Stout label left adhered to the peel plate, vacuum drum, brush assembly, or conveyor guide rails during changeover can transfer to a Pale Ale can after the roll is swapped.
* **Vendor Roll-Assembly Errors:** Label converters occasionally splice end-of-roll webbings or mix SKUs within a single roll. Without the camera reading 100% of barcodes/artwork, a spliced stout label will pass undetected.
* **FSMA & Recall Economics:** If a single lactose-labeled or lactose-containing can goes into a Pale Ale case and triggers an allergic reaction, FDA/TTB investigation will subpoena the line logs. Shipping across a known camera dropout during an allergen changeover without following SOP will be cited as a critical compliance failure.

---

## How to Meet the 06:00 Cut (The Inspection Math)

3,612 cans is approximately **150 cases (24-pack)** or **301 cases (12-pack)**. You do not need to destroy product if you organize a rapid QA sorting table—the labor math is completely on your side:

| Staffing Level | Inspection Rate (~1 can/sec) | Total Time Required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1 Operator** | 60 cans / min | ~60 minutes |
| **2 Operators** | 120 cans / min | ~30 minutes |
| **3 Operators** | 180 cans / min | **~20 minutes** |

### Execution Protocol

1. **Quarantine the Window:** Isolate the exact case-packer discharge pallet(s) covering the 3,612-can window using case timestamps or sequential case-packer counts.
2. **Establish a Rapid Sort Line:**
* Pull cases off the pallet onto a well-lit table.
* Check every individual can for **brand identity (Pale Ale)** and **barcode/SKU**.
* Repack into verified cases and mark the outer carton with a distinctive QA release stamp or tape.


3. **Document the Deviation:** Attach a signed **Allergen Deviation & 100% Inspection Record** to the batch record. List the camera downtime timestamps, the case count verified, the names of the inspectors, and the zero-defect result.

If Ops cannot spare 2–3 people for 20 minutes before 06:00, then **destroy or hold the pallet** for a later shipment. Never trade a 20-minute labor task for a Class I allergen recall.
